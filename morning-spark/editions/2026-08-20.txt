Lever size, the frame you never chose, and the cut loop.

─────────────────
01 · Empowerment has a size, and it is the number you may move
Jason Cohen × Marty Cagan
Cohen only stopped grinding on cancellation rate when someone asked what would actually be different if he won. Cagan says an empowered team owns an outcome rather than a backlog. Put them together and ownership gets a unit: the size of the swing you are allowed to attempt.
→ https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-priority/

─────────────────
02 · The frame you never chose is choosing your roadmap
April Dunford × Andrew Chen
Dunford's pitch contest story is a strategy story wearing marketing clothes: the frame of reference fixes price, market and roadmap long before anyone argues about a feature. Chen's cold start says the only market that counts at the beginning is the smallest one you can saturate. Together they turn positioning into a sizing question: not the biggest story you can tell, but the smallest room you can fill completely.
→ https://www.aprildunford.com/post/what-the-heck-is-your-product-really

─────────────────
03 · Your org chart is a funnel wearing a suit
Paul Graham × Brian Balfour
Graham says the modular org chart is what breaks founders' companies. Balfour says the funnel is what breaks growth, because it hands each layer to a different team with a different goal. They are the same mistake at two altitudes: a system that only works whole, managed as if it were parts.
→ https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html

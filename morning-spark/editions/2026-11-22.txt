Morning Spark · Sunday, November 22 · Ed. 94
Three lopsided things, and which side you are standing on.

─────────────────
01 · The router picks the cheapest bid. Privacy is not on the bid.
HD Moore × George Akerlof
Moore found the cheapest resellers were cheap because they logged the prompts. When quality cannot be seen, a low price is not a discount, it is a filter.
→ https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/

─────────────────
02 · A codebase never gets far enough ahead to afford a blunder
Sean Goedecke × Charlie Munger
Goedecke says a codebase has no lead to protect: one second source of truth undoes years of care. Agents are as consistent as you and worse at saying no to a requirement, so saying no is now the job.
→ https://seangoedecke.com/to-code-well-avoid-blunders/

─────────────────
03 · The model predicts the brain. The brain cannot predict the model.
Kohitij Kar × Alan Turing
A network that explains half the variance in the brain still carries units the brain cannot account for. A good fit says the model works, not that it works the way the thing does.
→ https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261120075701.htm

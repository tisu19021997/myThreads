01 · Autonomy follows the cost of being wrong
Ben Thompson × Paul Graham
Thompson's asymmetry is a general law, not a security one. Where being wrong is recoverable, autonomy arrives early. Where it is attributable, a human stays in the loop and the work stays human-speed.
→ https://stratechery.com/2026/autonomy-and-innovation/

─────────────────
02 · Better is meaningless until you name the alternative
Marty Cagan × April Dunford
Cagan is blunt that discovery is not there to confirm the problem is real; leaders already know that. It is there to prove a solution wins. Dunford's point is that winning is scored against a field somebody had to pick.
→ https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-faq/

─────────────────
03 · The metric that works is the awkward one
Andrew Chen × Jason Cohen
Chen's other one is the Decision to Rumination Ratio: months spent choosing a car, a week spent choosing a job. Stakes and thinking time are barely correlated, and nobody notices until the ratio has a name.
→ https://andrewchen.substack.com/p/lies-per-second-meetings-per-decision

Three defaults nobody chose, already shipped.

─────────────────
01 · An AI read of your team scores the paper trail
Camille Fournier × Gibson Biddle
Fournier's warning is that a summary quietly removes the signal you would have picked up in the room. Biddle's is that a proxy you did not choose is still one your team is managing to. Together they turn the AI digest into a performance review nobody sat down to write.
→ https://skamille.medium.com/the-managers-path-in-the-age-of-ai-279cb6611d66

─────────────────
02 · Agent capacity decays like an acquisition channel
Nathan Lambert × Andrew Chen
Lambert's argument is mostly about bottlenecks: narrow automatable research, diminishing returns from parallelism, and resource limits that do not care how many agents you run. Chen has been making the same shape of argument about growth channels for over a decade. Budget your agents like a channel and the honest question stops being how many, and starts being what they are waiting on.
→ https://www.interconnects.ai/p/where-i-stand-on-rsi

─────────────────
03 · Your repo's vocabulary drifted and nobody voted
Hillel Wayne × Teresa Torres
Wayne went looking for a verbal tic and came back with a measurement: spine up roughly twentyfold in pull request titles against overall growth near 1.5x. Torres's discipline is writing the assumption down before you test it. The join is uncomfortable, because a default you never noticed is still a default you shipped.
→ https://buttondown.com/hillelwayne/archive/the-llms-yearn-for-the-spines/

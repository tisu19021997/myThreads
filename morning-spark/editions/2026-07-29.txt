Three collisions on inherited curves, honest numbers, and chosen frames.

─────────────────
01 · Your ceiling was fixed before launch
Ben Thompson × Andrew Chen
Thompson's read is that today's price umbrella comes from scarce compute, and it hides whose cost structure is actually cheaper; when supply catches up, structure is all that is left. Chen's is that a D1 of ten percent is not a tuning problem, it is a product nobody wanted, and the honest move is to pivot in week one.
→ https://stratechery.com/2026/whos-afraid-of-chinese-models/

─────────────────
02 · A score with no reason gets ignored
Teresa Torres × Gibson Biddle
Hertility found clinicians adopted the model faster when it returned several diagnoses with probabilities and the evidence for each, rather than one confident label. Proxy metrics fail the same way: the number gets adopted company wide, the link to the value it stands for never gets tested.
→ https://www.producttalk.org/building-ai-for-womens-health-how-hertility-combined-bayesian-diagnosis-and-scan-automation/

─────────────────
03 · ARPU is a positioning decision
Brian Balfour × April Dunford
Balfour's threshold is brutally simple and almost nobody actually runs it: market size times willingness to pay times realistic capture. Dunford's half is that willingness to pay is not a property of the market, it is a property of the frame you put yourself in, which means the equation has one variable you get to author.
→ https://brianbalfour.com/essays/model-market-fit-threshold-for-growth

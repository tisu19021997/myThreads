Three safeguards still on the diagram, no longer in the loop.

─────────────────
01 · He shipped 11x. The review ratio went to zero.
Neil Kakkar × Peter Naur
Kakkar charted an 11x jump in merged pull requests and, on the same axes, a reviewed-to-merged ratio falling to nearly zero, with one person holding the context for each patch of the product. Naur worked out in 1985 what that ratio measures: a program's theory transfers only by close contact, never out of a document. The code is fine. The theory has a bus factor of one.
→ https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-software-engineering-is-changing

─────────────────
02 · Relabel the task and the model gets worse at it
Zvi Mowshowitz × Thorstein Veblen
Matched tasks, only the label changed: Astra finished about 10 percent of the hidden rubric under a safety framing and about 16 percent under a capabilities one, and an instruction to be careful made that gap half again as wide. Veblen named the mechanism in 1914, as abilities that function as blind spots. What is new is the direction. The incapacity sits in the very thing the training was for, so more of the training is not the fix.
→ https://thezvi.substack.com/p/gpt-6-astra-covertly-underperforms

─────────────────
03 · Ninety dollars for a 32-step break-in
Tomasz Tunguz × Gary Becker
The same 32-step network takeover cost 5,000 to 10,000 dollars in April and about 90 five months later, on open weights. Tunguz writes the attacker's decision as pure expected value on compute. Becker wrote that equation in 1968 with two more terms in it, the chance of being caught and the price of being caught, and called the second one cheap to move. Both are gone, so the only lever left is the one he called expensive.
→ https://tomtunguz.com/a-90-dollar-key-into-your-companys-secrets/

What surrounds the work decides what the work is worth.

─────────────────
01 · That no may be your context, not your idea
Teresa Torres × April Dunford
Torres wants twenty assumptions per idea, sorted by evidence and importance, so you test the risky ones instead of the whole thing. Dunford's Joshua Bell is the assumption nobody writes down: the frame. Add one line to the map, about what would have to be true of where you tested.
→ https://www.producttalk.org/cdh-book-club-august-2026/

─────────────────
02 · Work is a stage; your worth is not on it
John Cutler × Marty Cagan
Cutler borrows Goffman: work is front stage, and the feedback is a director's note, not a verdict. Cagan's version is colder, big hat and no cattle, and the hat is the first thing cut. Put your self-worth in the craft and your patience in the scene, not the other way round.
→ https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-429-how-to-thrive-at-work-when

─────────────────
03 · If an agent can do the hour, the hour was never the asset
Jason Cohen × Gibson Biddle
Cohen's $1000 an hour was an argument for delegating everything small. AI answered it: the small things are free now. But a freed hour is not a won hour. Biddle's filter is the one that still charges you. Put it into what is hard to copy, or you have automated your way to the same place faster.
→ https://longform.asmartbear.com/value-time/

What only a room confirmed is not evidence.

─────────────────
01 · Your retention curve is a verdict, not a diagnosis
Teresa Torres × Andrew Chen
A bad curve is a verdict, and Chen says you cannot A/B test your way out of one. Torres says the cheap signals feel like they tell you what to build and almost never do. The pivot is inside the story of someone who churned, and nobody in your inbox is them.
→ https://www.producttalk.org/quality-of-evidence-all-things-product-podcast-with-teresa-torres-petra-wille/

─────────────────
02 · AI buys two legs of the stool, never the third
Brian Balfour × Gibson Biddle
Balfour's LinkedIn loop went from zero to a million monthly uniques by using AI to shrink the gap between reading and creating. Nobody claims the content is good. Biddle's test asks which of delight, hard to copy and margin you actually bought, and this one bought two.
→ https://brianbalfour.com/essays/a-breakdown-of-linkedins-ai-assisted-growth-loop

─────────────────
03 · Stop reading the deck, read what got funded
Melissa Perri × April Dunford
Perri's move when strategy is fuzzy is to stop asking and start reading: what got funded, what got killed, what got celebrated. Dunford's warning is the twin of it, a structured story built on invented inputs sells beautifully and is still fiction. Both of them trade the document for the record.
→ https://melissaperri.com/blog/2024/10/22/how-to-get-clarity-when-your-companys-strategy-is-fuzzy

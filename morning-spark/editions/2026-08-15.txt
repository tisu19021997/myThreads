Three times the real decision sits outside the frame.

─────────────────
01 · Your recurring fight is the honest metric
John Cutler × Gibson Biddle
Cutler argues teams prioritize better by watching the tensions they keep re-enacting than by scoring options. Biddle argues you steer by a proxy metric that reports back early. Put together: the fight you keep having reports back earliest of all, and it is free.
→ https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-436-tension-based-prioritization

─────────────────
02 · A strategy doc that surprises nobody is output
Paul Graham × Melissa Perri
Graham says an essay exists to change thinking, and the writer's own thinking first. Perri says strategy is only real when someone can act on it. Both point at the same test, and most strategy docs fail it: nothing in them was discovered by writing them.
→ https://paulgraham.com/field.html

─────────────────
03 · The danger zone moved, and not by your hand
Brian Balfour × Ben Thompson
Balfour showed that your model picks your channel, and that the middle of the ARPU spectrum kills companies. Thompson showed that whoever owns demand sets the terms. When the channel becomes an agent, pricing stops being a growth decision you make and becomes one made about you.
→ https://brianbalfour.com/essays/channel-model-fit-for-user-acquisition
